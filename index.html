Why this scan probably overstates the damage Total body water dropped 2.4 L in a month — a much bigger swing than fat or muscle actually change that fast. InBody estimates muscle and fat from how water conducts through you, so a scan taken more dehydrated, at a different time of day, or after travel/alcohol/a heavier meal than your last one will read "more fat, less muscle" even if your body hasn't really changed that much. A week of holiday travel — different sleep, more sitting, alcohol, saltier food, less consistent water — is exactly the kind of week that produces a lower body-water reading. This doesn't mean nothing happened; it means don't take 2.6 kg of fat gained and 2.0 kg of muscle lost at face value from one scan.
What to actually do about it Two things, not one panicked overhaul: (1) get a clean re-scan, same time of day, similarly hydrated, ideally fasted — that tells you how much of this was real versus water, and how the restructured split below is actually landing. (2) tighten up regardless, because "not much junk" during a disrupted week almost always still means more alcohol, more eating out, more sitting, and less sleep than a normal week, even when it doesn't feel like it.

The logic: Push and Pull run back to back early in the week while you're freshest, then Wednesday's rest breaks the week before fatigue compounds. Warm-up & stretching

A warm-up has four jobs, in this order: raise your body temperature, open the joints you're about to use, switch on the muscles you need, then rehearse the first lift before it gets heavy. 5–8 minutes, every session, no skipping it on a rushed day — that's when a cold shoulder gets hurt.

Dynamic before, static after Everything below the workout heading is dynamic — movement-based. Save deep, held stretches for after training (see cool-down at the bottom): holding a long static stretch on a cold, unloaded muscle right before lifting can temporarily reduce the strength and power you can produce in that same session.

Training split

Rebuilt from the Push / Pull / Legs / Upper / Lower sheet you sent over, kept as the backbone — same weekly order, same exercise families where they were already shoulder-safe (pull-ups, rows, face pulls, squats, RDLs, hip thrusts). Two things from that sheet were swapped out and one was reintroduced carefully — see the callout below for exactly what changed and why. Progression: once every set hits the top of its rep range with clean form, add the smallest weight increment available next session. Any pinching, sharp, or radiating pain — stop that exercise, drop the load, or swap in the regression noted.

What changed from the source sheet, and why Bench press (Push and Upper days) — removed entirely, replaced with neutral-grip dumbbell or cable pressing. This is the one you flagged yourself; no change in reasoning from the original plan. Weighted dips (Push day) — removed, replaced with standing cable chest press. Dips drive the shoulder into a deep, loaded stretch at the bottom, a common trigger for exactly the kind of pain you've described. Dumbbell shoulder press — kept, but deliberately lighter and partial-range on both Push and Upper days, marked "testing" in the tables below. This is a controlled re-introduction, not the sheet's prescribed working weight: go light, stop well short of full overhead lockout, and if it's sore the next day, drop it again and stick to the landmine press and lateral raise work instead.
Clean, proportionate, not bulky Two different rep ranges doing two different jobs, on purpose. The big compounds — presses, rows, squats, RDLs, hip thrusts — stay heavier and lower-rep (6–10): that's what actually builds the shape of an athletic, proportionate body, and it does it without piling on size the way high-volume training does. Arm and shoulder accessory work — curls, triceps, lateral raises, rear delts — is deliberately kept at 2 sets and a higher 12–15 reps: enough to bring out definition in the arms and shoulders, not enough volume to add bulk there. "Good arms" here means visible, not big. Most days also carry one explosive or carry-based movement — kettlebell swings, box jumps, broad jumps — which does more for an athletic look than another isolation set would. Push and Upper skip that extra power move, since both are already pressing-heavy days for a shoulder you're managing carefully. The "lean torso" part of this isn't a chest or ab exercise — it's the calorie deficit and cardio day doing their job; see Belly & chest fat.

Shoulder care

This split avoids the movements most likely to aggravate a painful shoulder, but it isn't a diagnosis or treatment. The routine below is a mobility sequence adapted from DAREBEE's rotator-cuff work — run it before every Push and Upper day, and ideally daily.

Move slowly, well inside a pain-free range — this builds control of the small rotator-cuff muscles, not visible size. Consistency beats intensity here.

See a physiotherapist or sports doctor if Pain is sharp or pinching rather than a dull ache · it wakes you at night · you lose strength or range lifting the arm overhead · pain radiates down toward the elbow. Any of these are worth a proper shoulder assessment before you add load — this plan can't substitute for that, and pushing through those signs is how a manageable issue becomes a structural one. This matters more now that the dumbbell shoulder press is back in, even lightly.

Belly & chest fat

Your segmental data flags exactly what you're describing: trunk fat is proportionally much higher than arm or leg fat on every scan so far. That's a genuine pattern — central fat storage is common in South Asian body types even at a "normal" BMI, and it's what's behind your visceral fat level, now at 11 and above the 1–9 reference range on the InBody sheet since the Sep 11 scan.

  • Spot reduction doesn't work — no ab or chest exercise burns fat from that specific area. Overall body-fat % has to drop, and the trunk tends to be the last place it visibly leaves.
  • Visceral fat responds disproportionately well to the basics: a consistent calorie deficit, daily steps/movement (not just gym days), resistance training to protect muscle while you cut, 7–8 hours of sleep, and lower stress.
  • Cut added sugar and alcohol before cutting carbs generally — both are strongly linked to visceral fat specifically, more than total carbohydrate intake is.
  • Chest training (the incline and cable press work in this plan) builds and firms the muscle under the fat, which noticeably improves shape as body fat drops — but it can't remove fat tissue sitting on top of it. Expect chest shape to improve steadily alongside overall fat loss, not overnight.
  • If you ever notice a firm, coin-sized lump specifically behind the nipple rather than general softness — that's a medical question (possible gynecomastia), not a training one. Worth mentioning to a doctor, not something to try to train away.

Fibre ≥30g/day, water ~3–3.5L/day. Don't go lower than this on calories to chase faster results — a deficit steeper than this is what actually risks losing more muscle, which is the opposite of what this reset is for. Recalculate this block from your next InBody's basal metabolism figure.

Travel & holiday days On a trip, don't try to hit these exact numbers. Instead, protect two things only — protein (even 120g/day from eggs, curd, chicken, a shaker bottle) and steps — and let calories and food quality flex. Cap alcohol at a couple of drinks on the heaviest nights rather than cutting it entirely (more sustainable than an all-or-nothing rule), and if there's no gym, the warm-up bodyweight circuits in this plan work as a 15-minute hotel-room session.

Steps

Check your phone's 7-day average, then add 1,000 steps/day until you're at 8,000–12,000. On a genuinely packed day, 7,000 is the floor, not zero — daily movement is one of the lowest-risk levers you have, independent of whatever the gym split looks like.

Tracking it

  • Get a fresh InBody scan soon — it's been well over two months since Sep 11, longer than planned. You need one both to see how the reversal actually settled and as the baseline for this restructured five-day split.
  • Same conditions every time — same time of day, similarly hydrated, ideally fasted and before training, not after. The Aug→Sep swing shows how much scan conditions alone can move these numbers.
  • Waist at the navel, fortnightly, same time of day — a better week-to-week signal than the scale, and unaffected by hydration-driven InBody swings.
  • Photos — front, side, back — fortnightly, same lighting and pose.
  • Training log — weight × reps on the main lift of each day, so "add the smallest increment" actually gets tracked. Worth also logging soreness (1–10) and sleep hours alongside it, the way the sheet's own progress tracker does — those two predict a bad session better than anything else.
  • Steps and sleep — both move visceral fat more than people expect.

Judge any single scan against the trend, not in isolation — your own three data points so far show both what's possible (Jul→Aug) and how easily a disrupted week shows up in the numbers (Aug→Sep). Don't overreact to one, don't ignore the other.
